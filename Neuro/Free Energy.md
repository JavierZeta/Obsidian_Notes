
First formulation
$$
F = -\langle \ln p(\tilde{s}, \vartheta | m) \rangle_{q} + \langle \ln q(\vartheta | \mu) \rangle_{q}

$$

This is the first definition, where:

- s is the sensation
- $\vartheta$ is the hidden cause behind that sensation
- m is the model of my brain, how my brain understand all the physical world.
- $\mu$ is the state of my brain, biologically speaking



- q is the probability function of the cause in my current state. The sensation enters I give it a possible cause and then my brain surprise about that cause in my current state

- p is the probability function of the sensation due to the cause, it doesnt include my state at all. It comes in pair sensation&cause 

Then we do the <>q  thing that is multiplying each cause with each probability and sum them to get the scalar that will be the free energy, because you dont have only one posible cause.



Second Formulation
$$
F = D(q(\vartheta | \mu) \parallel p(\vartheta)) - \langle \ln p(\tilde{s}(a) | \vartheta, m) \rangle_{q}
$$
Here D is the Kullback-Leibler divergence, that measures the distance between, in this case, my current believe q and my previous schema. It measures how far I'm im my current believe from my priors.

In the second term, a is an action, that produces the sensation. I make actions in order to reduce my free energy, if the sensation originated from my action has a cause that can be explained by my model I reduced free-energy.



Third  Formulation 
$$
F = D(q(\vartheta | \mu) \parallel p(\vartheta | \tilde{s})) - \ln p(\tilde{s} | m)
$$

In the first term we have the difference between my guess and the perfect guess. The brain is permatly seeking that perfect guess but....
The brain cannot know the perfect guess, only GOD, then how ? 
The brain don't know the final position but feels the gradient, art. That gradient is calculated by checking the difference between what it feels and what it should feel.  

The second term is the surprise, how well my model explains the sensation, it cannot be reduced because it does not depend on  $\mu$ .
